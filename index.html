What goes inside

First model: the hardware

  • Hidden flexible AMOLED screen behind the casing on all four sides. AMOLED pixels light themselves, so no backlight is needed. When it is off, the speaker is one clean object; when it is on, the words glow through.
  • Turning base with a quiet motor that slowly turns the speaker, so everyone in the room can read it. The base stays still.
  • A grid of small colour LEDs behind the casing, each one its own colour: it colours the casing and draws soft, slowly moving patterns of light (flowers, mandala, ribbons, stars, girih, arabesque) in one colour or many, chosen in the app. Nothing is printed.
  • Microphones on top, so you can say "Assalamu alaikum, Ustadh, play Surah Yasin".
  • Touch top with play, previous and next ayah, and volume, around a light ring that shows listening and thinking.
  • Underneath: a down-facing speaker behind a round grille, four rubber feet, USB-C and a power button that cuts the power completely.
  • Standby from the app, the remote or by voice: screen and lights off, one tiny dim light on the ring. It can wake itself for the adhan.
  • A Bluetooth remote with 12 buttons for anyone without the app: standby, previous, play/pause, next, volume down and up, repeat ayah, mode, translation language, light, talk and turn-to-face-me. It has its own small microphone, so you can talk from across the room without raising your voice. Bluetooth works from any side, which matters for a speaker that turns.
  • Wi-Fi for recitations and translations, Bluetooth for a phone, battery, no fan.

What it does

  • Recite, translate, explain in Urdu, Arabic or English, from named sources, with the reference said or shown.
  • Bayans from an approved list of scholars, with their own published translation on one side of the screen.
  • Screen layouts: a running line, Arabic and translation sides, the same on every side, or four different sides.
  • Holy places and stories on the screen: line art of the two Harams and Al-Aqsa, Stories of the Prophets in words.
  • Prayer times and adhan for your city, with a gentle light a few minutes before each prayer.
  • Hifz mode, night mode with Surah Al-Mulk, respectful mode, and a kids mode with the Kiitab students.
  • Phone app to set up the speaker, choose its voice and personality, and pick what each side shows.

From the simplest to the most advanced

Every model shares the same bottom half. Only the top and the screen change, so each step up is a new top, not a new speaker.

  • Step 1, Plain: no screen. It speaks, and its light shows what it is doing.
  • Step 2, Face: one flexible AMOLED screen on the front, which shows the face and turns with the body.
  • Step 3, Wrap: the flexible screen goes all the way round, so the words can be read from any side.
  • Step 4, Cube: a cube fully covered with AMOLED on all four sides. The most advanced, with the most power, heat and cost, so it comes last.
  • Speaker: a new model, not a step on the ladder. A modern speaker with one full AMOLED side, speakers on the other sides so the voice is clear, operation buttons on top, and a strong base that does not turn.

AMOLED burns in if one picture stays still for hours, so the face moves and dims over time.

Later models

  • Projector model: a projector inside shows the text on milky panels all around. Premium version.
  • Qibla direction on the screen, with a compass chip inside.
  • Same brain as the app: the 3D speaker on a phone or laptop behaves exactly like the real one.

Never on this speaker

No music and no musical instruments, in any mode. No songs, no background tunes, no jingles, not even a startup tune: only recitation, the spoken voice and soft clicks. Nothing haram on the screen or in the voice: no pictures of the prophets or the companions, no ads. Stories are told in words. The rule is written into the speaker's character file, so the brain cannot switch music on even if someone asks for it.

Shapes, patterns and the control tool

Classic is a rounded square framed by wood at the top and the base, with thin brass rings around the hidden screen. Mihrab narrows gently towards the top like a lantern. In both, the screen is one whole screen all the way round; mihrab arches can be drawn on it around what each side shows.

Simpler screens, lower prices. Front screen has one solid flat screen on the front only, framed in brass, so the light inside never shows through it, and plain fabric on the other three sides: it costs less and suits a shelf against a wall. Small display has no screen, only a little dot-matrix LCD on the front with two short lines (surah and ayah, next prayer, the time, the dhikr count), so it never shows the verse itself. Plain has no screen at all: it says everything out loud, and its light ring and the light inside the casing show listening, reciting and prayer time. All three keep the same casing, light patterns, add-ons, cover and app.

Patterns are light, not print. A grid of small LEDs behind the casing draws stars, girih or arabesque, softened by the fabric or frosted plastic. They change from the app and disappear when the light is off. For a different colour or texture, the casing itself can be a slip-on cover held by magnets.

A cover you can change. The fabric (linen, cotton or canvas) or frosted plastic is a sleeve with the screen's brass borders attached. Twist off the top section, slide the old sleeve up and off, and slide on a new one; the bottom section comes off the same way for cleaning. The screen and the frame inside never move, so a new cover is cheap and safe to fit at home.

Add-ons on top. A dome in the style of Masjid an-Nabawi, four minarets, both, a golden dome, the Kaaba on its mataf, or a Ramadan lantern. Each has its own lights inside. Each clicks onto the wooden top with magnets and a few metal contacts that power its lights, so the dome glows softly with the speaker's light and the minarets' balconies light up. They are sold separately, so a family can change the look of the speaker over the years.

Topics and the microphone. The cards in the app are topics: Recite, Bayan, Dhikr, Qaida, Stories and more, and later Arabic grammar, vocabulary and a discussion topic. Tap one and the speaker asks what you want (for Recite: which surah, which ayahs or which subject, and which translation). To talk to it, tap the microphone in the app or on the remote: the phone or the remote is the microphone, so you never have to shout over the recitation. While you talk the speaker turns itself down and its own microphone is off, and it understands what you say only within the topic that is on, which makes it far less likely to mishear. It stops listening by itself when you stop talking. A few things always work: stop, louder, softer, standby, and "go to" another topic.

One control tool. The Kiitab app beside the speaker is the same tool everywhere: next to the 3D speaker on the desktop, as the phone app, and for the real speaker over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth. Every button sends the same message to the brain, so the speaker never needs to be touched.

All text uses the Mushaf-style Quran script with the traditional ayah markers, and warm white is the first light colour.
